How to Calculate Paint Coverage Accurately

Calculating how much paint you need requires finding the net surface area of your walls. First, multiply the room's total perimeter by the wall height to find the total wall space. Subtract the square footage of any doors and windows to get your net painting area. On average, one gallon of interior paint covers 350 square feet, while one liter covers approximately 10 square meters.

1 Coat vs. 2 Coats: How Much Paint Do You Need?

While standard calculations display coverage for a single coat, most interior and exterior painting projects require at least two coats for full opacity, durability, and smooth finish. If you are painting over a dark color with a lighter shade, apply a primer first or plan for a third coat to achieve even color representation.

Wall Surface Texture and Paint Absorption

The material of your wall heavily influences total coverage. Smooth, pre-primed drywall absorbs paint evenly and yields maximum coverage. Unprimed drywall, textured stucco, or brick absorbs significantly more liquid, requiring 10% to 25% more paint than calculated standard surface estimates.
